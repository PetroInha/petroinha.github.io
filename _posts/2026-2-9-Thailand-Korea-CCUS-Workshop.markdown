<h2>Thailand–Korea CCUS Joint Research Workshop 🌏</h2>
<p>
About forty researchers from Korea and Thailand met in Bangkok for three days of CCUS
exchange — a full day of research presentations at Chulalongkorn University, a lab tour
at PTTEP's Subsurface Research Center, and a student research network session to close
things out.
</p>

  <span class="post-chip">📅 February 9–11, 2026</span>

  <span class="post-chip">🌏 Korea × Thailand</span>
  <span class="post-chip">🌱 CCUS Research Exchange</span>
</div>
</div>

<div class="data-flow"></div>

Carbon capture, utilization and storage is a problem neither country solves alone. Korea
has been building CCS capability against a hard national emissions target with very
limited domestic storage; Thailand sits on depleted gas fields and an operator, PTTEP,
already moving on offshore CO₂ storage. The two research agendas complement each other
almost too neatly to ignore — which is exactly why this workshop was organized.

The three days were structured around a simple idea: put both research groups in the
same room, let everyone present what they are actually working on, and then go look at
the facilities where that work gets done.

A deliberately balanced group — roughly forty people in total, split evenly between the
two countries, and mixing university researchers, a national energy policy institute,
and an operating E&amp;P company. That mix mattered: the same storage question sounds
different depending on whether you are simulating it, regulating it, or drilling it.

<div class="visit-box">
<h4>🛢️ Inside an operator's subsurface facility</h4>
<p>
The second day was given over entirely to PTTEP's Subsurface Research Center (PSRC) — a
morning bus ride out of Bangkok, a full lab tour, and the ride back. For the students in
particular this was the part that is hard to replicate in a classroom: the core storage,
the analytical instruments, and the data infrastructure that an operating company
actually runs its subsurface decisions on.
</p>
<p>
Seeing how core, log, and seismic data are curated and turned into decisions at an
E&amp;P company is a useful corrective for anyone who spends most of their time on the
simulation side of the same problem. Our own work on digital rock physics and AI-driven
subsurface modeling starts from data that someone, somewhere, had to acquire and
organize like this.
</p>
