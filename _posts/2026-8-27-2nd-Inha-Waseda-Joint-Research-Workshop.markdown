<h2>2nd Inha × Waseda Joint Research Workshop 🌏</h2>
<p>
The Inha–Waseda collaboration returned for its second edition — this time hosted at
Waseda University in Tokyo, with faculty and graduate students from both universities
spending two days presenting their work and building the connections that make a joint
research program more than a memorandum.
</p>
<div class="post-chips">
  <span class="post-chip">📅 August 27–28, 2026</span>
  <span class="post-chip">📍 Waseda University, Tokyo</span>
  <span class="post-chip">🇯🇵 Inha × Waseda</span>
  <span class="post-chip">🤝 2nd Edition</span>
</div>
</div>

<div class="data-flow"></div>

A year on from the first workshop in Seoul, the two groups met again — this time on
Waseda's home ground. Reciprocal hosting is a small detail that says something real about
a collaboration: the first edition could have been a one-off, and the second is what makes
it a series.

investigators, one on each side, sharing responsibility for the agenda and for what the
collaboration is supposed to produce.

<div class="sec-head">
  <div class="sec-num">03</div>
  <h3>The shared research agenda</h3>
</div>

Since the first workshop the two groups have been working across a common set of
subsurface energy problems — the ground on which the joint program is built.

<div class="theme-grid">
  <div class="theme">
    <div class="theme-icon">🌱</div>
    <p class="theme-name">CO₂ storage</p>
    <p class="theme-desc">Capacity assessment, plume behaviour and long-term containment in saline aquifers and depleted reservoirs.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="theme">
    <div class="theme-icon">💧</div>
    <p class="theme-name">Subsurface hydrogen storage</p>
    <p class="theme-desc">Storing hydrogen underground at scale — a different fluid, and a different set of containment and cycling problems.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="theme">
    <div class="theme-icon">🪨</div>
    <p class="theme-name">Geomechanics</p>
    <p class="theme-desc">How the rock responds to injection: stress changes, seal integrity and induced deformation.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="theme">
    <div class="theme-icon">🤖</div>
    <p class="theme-name">AI-driven subsurface modeling</p>
    <p class="theme-desc">Machine learning applied to reservoir characterization, forecasting and uncertainty quantification.</p>
  </div>

  <h3>Mostly students, deliberately</h3>
</div>

<div class="callout">
<h4>👥 Where a collaboration actually lives</h4>
<p>
Look at the group photo and the majority of the faces are graduate students. That is the
point. Faculty agreements establish a collaboration; it survives because students on both
sides know each other's work, compare methods over lunch, and end up co-authoring
something nobody planned in advance. Two editions in, the Inha–Waseda program has the
beginnings of exactly that.
</p>
</div>
